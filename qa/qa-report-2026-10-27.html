Verdict

The app reproduces the thesis. Every factor of safety the thesis reports is matched to its printed precision, and the solver converges to exact solutions where they exist. The QA found one minor software bug and two gaps in what the app reports; none of them changes a factor of safety. The main risks for real-life use lie in the analysis set-up: the prescribed sphere is not the critical surface, and two published benchmarks are still unresolved.

Findings

Thirteen findings, most important first. The ID gives the area: S software, T thesis text, B benchmarks, R real-life use. The software fixes are small and can be made on the branch on request.

  1. R1▲HighReal-life use✓Addressed

    The prescribed sphere is not the critical surface

    A Nelder–Mead search over centre and radius, run with the app's own solver, finds lower factors of safety near the thesis's main sphere in every case: 13% lower in dry 2D, 16% in dry 3D, 9.5% in wet 2D and 15% in wet 3D. The critical wet 2D FoS is 0.99 for EJC (1.00 at 640 slices) and 0.94 for GHB. On these critical surfaces the 3D/2D ratio is 1.067 dry and 1.088 wet, against 1.105 and 1.159 on the main sphere. The same search lands within 4 m of the Carranza-Torres & Hormazábal circle, so the solver behaves correctly. The main sphere is simply not critical, and the thesis already names a critical-surface search as required before design use.

    Action. Report FoS on the critical surface, or state on every result that it applies to the prescribed sphere only. A search study could be added to the app.

    Addressed (commit 4818c53). Both apps now search for the critical circle (2D) and the critical sphere centred on y = 0 (3D) in their Studies, and can make either the prescribed surface. On the thesis slope its minima are at or below this page's in all eight cases: EJC 2.1186 and 2.2600 dry, 0.9929 and 1.0770 wet; GHB 2.0002 and 2.1359 dry, 0.9369 and 1.0214 wet. It tries only circles that meet the ground at both exits, because the engines accept a circle whose arc ends below the ground at its back extreme and give the mass a vertical back face with no strength (on Taylor's 75° slope, 1.6245 for a 2.8 m face against 1.6906 for the critical circle); the results summary of both apps now warns about such a face. It re-solves each critical surface on a finer mesh and warns above the 0.5% threshold; the wet cases exceed it at the default meshes (+0.8% to +1.8%).

  2. R2▲HighReal-life use

    Blast damage and pore pressure dominate the result

    The benchmark uses D = 0. Hoek, Carranza-Torres & Corkum (2002) suggest D = 1.0 for large open-pit slopes under production blasting and 0.7 where excavation is mechanical. With a taken from GSI, the app's 3D FoS on the main sphere falls from 2.62 at D = 0 to 1.73 at D = 0.7 and 1.27 at D = 1. The app applies D to the whole mass, while Hoek & Brown (2019) advise applying it only within the blast-damaged zone, so D = 1 throughout is a lower bound. Pore pressure is the other large lever: 3D FoS falls from 2.62 at ru = 0 to 1.06 at ru = 0.4.

    Action. Run D and drawdown as explicit sensitivity cases for any real pit, ideally on the critical surface.

  3. S3●MediumSoftware✓Fixed

    Accepted results can have most bases in tension, and the summary does not say so

    At ru = 0.5 on the thesis slope the run is accepted with FoS 0.56 (3D) and 0.41 (2D), yet 82% of 3D bases and every 2D base carry tensile effective normal stress. On the thesis's wet 80×80 mesh, 30% of bases are tensile at an accepted FoS of 1.28. The acceptance checks test the numerics, so they pass. The summary panel does not report the tensile count; it appears only as a colour in the 3D view and in the CSV export.

    Action. Show the number and share of tensile bases in the summary and flag results above a chosen share. For wet cases, pair the FoS with the C0 and T0 runs, as the thesis does in Table 5.7.

    Fixed (commit 38a3a2e). The summary of both apps now lists the tensile bases in 3D and 2D, with a note when there are any and a warning above a quarter of the bases.

  4. B1●MediumBenchmark

    D'Esterre's 1:3.5 slope: the Slide2 values behave like Ordinary-method results

    On the 16 circles on the 1:1.5 and 1:2.5 slopes, the app's Bishop FoS is within 0.2% of Slide2. On the 1:3.5 slope it is 3.4% to 8.2% higher on 7 of 8 circles. Moving the circle does not close the gap: the app's own critical circle nearby is still 3.2% to 6.6% higher. On the same slices, the Ordinary method of slices reproduces the four dry 1:3.5 values within 0.5%, and three of the four ru = 0.5 values within 0.6% (with N′ = (W − ub) cos α). The 1:3.5 values may therefore come from a different method column in D'Esterre's tables.

    Action. Check D'Esterre's Slide2 files before using the 1:3.5 rows as Bishop references. Until then, treat them as unconfirmed.

  5. B2●MediumBenchmark

    Law's case 4 (wet) is 9% to 14% below Law and Slide2

    The app gives 0.951 (640 slices) against Law's Excel 1.05 to 1.06 and Slide2 1.11. The three dry Law cases are within 2% of Slide2 and within 1% of Law's Excel. The app's wet pore pressure equals ru = γw/γ = 0.363 exactly; matching Slide2's 1.11 would need ru ≈ 0.31, and Law's 1.05 about 0.33. Law and Slide2 model a 12-bench profile with the water table on the benched surface, while the app uses the overall 50° profile. That difference is the most likely cause, but it cannot be confirmed without Law's model.

    Action. Obtain Law's Slide2 model, or rebuild the benched profile, before using case 4 as a wet benchmark.

  6. R3●MediumReal-life use

    Mechanisms outside the model

    The app has no seismic (pseudo-static) load, tension crack, surcharge or other external load, structurally controlled failure (joints, faults, anisotropy), non-spherical surface, multiple materials, or transient and perched groundwater. The tensile contact treatment is uncalibrated, as §5.8 of the thesis says.

    Action. State these exclusions wherever results are quoted, and check structurally controlled mechanisms separately.

  7. S1○LowSoftware bug✓Fixed

    The original-closure minimum at ν = 0.49 is 2.2 MPa high

    The app returns σ′n,min = 59,953.87 MPa at σ′3 = 58,770.58 MPa. The exact boundary minimum, solved at 50 digits, is 59,951.66 MPa at σ′3 = 58,768.41 MPa, so the app is 0.004% high. The thesis value, 59,952 MPa, is correct. The cause is in originalClosureMinimum: the golden-section refinement can end on a midpoint just outside the admissible boundary, which is then discarded, and the result falls back to the nearest grid point (about 8 MPa apart at this stress). At ν = 0.271 and 0.45 the final midpoint happened to be admissible and the results are exact. No factor of safety uses this function.

    Action. Keep the admissible end of the final bracket when the midpoint is rejected, in engine.js and engine.py, and add a ν = 0.49 regression test.

    Fixed (commit 38a3a2e). The search now keeps the best admissible point of its final bracket. At ν = 0.49 it returns 59,951.6628 MPa, and it matches the 50-digit edge minima at every ν of Table 5.2 to about 10−13. Both engines have regression tests, and Table 5.2 at ν = 0.49 is now a verification row.

  8. S2○LowSoftware✓Fixed

    The R ≤ zc refusal does not mention the base-circle option

    The default grid refuses circles whose lowest point is above the toe level with “require R > z_c”. Base-circle mode solves them: a 20 m, 60° test slope with a face circle gives an accepted FoS of 1.476. The other refusal, for circles that cross the toe level behind the toe, already suggests base-circle mode. Taylor's 60° toe circle needs it, and there the app gives 1.9434659 against the exact 1.9434661.

    Action. Add “use the base-circle option” to the R ≤ zc message.

    Fixed (commit 38a3a2e). The message now ends “for a face circle, with its lowest point above the toe level, use the base-circle option”, and the base-circle hint mentions face circles.

  9. T1○LowThesis text

    Route 1 residuals are half of rEJC

    The residuals −0.63, −1.09 and −1.80 MPa in Table 5.1, and the 0.63–1.80 MPa in the Project Summary, §4.3 and §6, equal σ′1 − σ′1,EJC from eq. 2.8 at the state's σ′2 and σ′3. That is exactly half of rEJC in eqs A.1 and B.27, which the §4.6 acceptance test uses. In rEJC form they are −1.26, −2.17 and −3.59 MPa. The conclusion that Route 1 fails is unchanged.

    Action. Say which form is reported, or report rEJC.

  10. T2○LowThesis text

    The stated EJC tolerance differs from the listed code

    §4.6 requires the unsquared EJC to hold to 10−8 MPa. The VBA in Appendix D uses 10−8 · max(1, σci), which is 7.8 × 10−7 MPa at σci = 77.7 MPa, and the Python listing uses 10−9 · max(1, σci, |σ′1|). Recorded residuals, below 3 × 10−14 MPa, pass all three, so no result changes. The app applies 10−8 MPa as §4.6 states.

    Action. Align §4.6 and the code.

  11. T3○LowThesis text

    Five small wording and rounding items

    • §5.5 says FM is “approximately 0.14–0.27%” below FH. Table 5.5 and the recomputation give 0.14–0.26%, with a largest difference of 0.263%.
    • Fig. 5.8 shows −24.23% for C0 against EJC (wet 3D). That comes from the rounded FoS 0.9640 and 1.2723; unrounded values give −24.25%.
    • Table C.2's 2D values, 2.447910895 and 1.098069496, look truncated. The app gives 2.4479108955 and 1.0980694965; the 3D values agree to every printed digit.
    • §5.1 says local residuals were “below 10−15 of column weight”. The app gets 6 × 10−16 dry and up to 5.7 × 10−15 wet on the native path. Both are rounding error, so “of order 10−15” is safer.
    • §5.4 gives the tangent shear-strength gain as “about 5.9–6.6%”. At the accepted 20×20 and 40-slice states the app gives 5.95–6.43%.

    Action. Adjust the wording.

  12. B3○LowBenchmark

Thesis reproduction

Every value in the thesis that the app can compute was recomputed with webapp/engine.js: 193 values from the Project Summary, Chapters 2, 4 and 5, and Appendices A to C. A value matches when it lies within half a unit of its last printed digit; counts and statuses must match exactly. The app has no Route 1, so the Route 1 columns of Table 5.1 come from an independent rebuild of eqs 4.8, 4.9 and 4.5, which reproduces φ′i, c′i and τs exactly. The Excel workbook was not available, so the comparison is against the printed values.

Also confirmed

  • Table 5.2 at ν = 0.49: the analytical 59,952 MPa is right (59,951.66 MPa at 50 digits).
  • The Appendix A.5 and B.5 states agree with the Wolfram listing in D.1 to about 10−14; the B.5 state is identical to every digit.
  • Root order: across 176,231 scanned states, both roots of the squared EJC were never admissible at once, so trying the minus root first (Appendix D) or the plus root first (the app) cannot change a result.
  • The native iteration path converges in 34 and 63 iterations for the dry and wet baselines, as §5.1 and Appendix C.2 report.

Independent checks

These checks do not rely on the thesis numbers. They test the solver against exact solutions, a classical chart, a second implementation and high-precision arithmetic.

Exact solutions for φ = 0

For a purely cohesive material, Bishop's method is exact moment equilibrium on a circle, and on a sphere the moment form FM is exact. The Hungr form FH has its own continuum limit. All three were integrated directly from the geometry by adaptive quadrature, with no app code, for three surfaces: the thesis's main sphere, the Carranza-Torres & Hormazábal circle, and D'Esterre's deepest base circle (1:3.5, c4). The error falls as the mesh is refined, with the scatter that §5.6 describes as columns enter and leave the active domain. At the finest meshes 2D is within 0.007% (10,000 slices) and 3D within 0.08% (320 × 320).

Randomised tests

Random inputs in two ranges, 400 input sets each, every set solved in 3D and 2D: 1,600 runs in all. The realistic range covers slope heights of 2–20 m and 50–600 m, β 25–70°, σci 10–250 MPa and GSI 15–95 across EJC, GHB, σ′2 = σ′3 and Mohr-Coulomb, with dry, water-table and ru conditions and all tension and iteration options. The extreme range widens these to H 0.5–1,000 m, β 10–89°, σci 1–300 MPa, a 0.3–1.0, ru up to 0.99 and meshes down to 2. Each run was solved by the app (JavaScript) and by the Python engine. Every accepted result was then re-checked from its returned arrays with numpy: column geometry, the unsquared EJC, closure and stress order at each base, local vertical equilibrium (eq. 4.12), global FH or FM (eqs 4.14, 4.15) and Bishop (eq. 4.16).

Refused inputs are circles the grid does not support, mostly face circles (see S2). Withheld runs stop with a stated reason: no admissible state, non-positive mα or trial F, or a failed final check. No run crashed or returned a non-finite FoS. Limits: local residual 10−9, EJC 10−8 MPa.

50-digit local strength

313 accepted local states from 150 random strength sets (EJC with both closures, and GHB) were solved again at 50 digits by direct root finding on the closure. The worst relative differences are 2.6 × 10−14 in φ′i, 3.4 × 10−15 in c′i and 8.4 × 10−16 in τs. In 136 cases neither method found a state. In one case only the app did; that state is genuine, since at 50 digits it satisfies the closure and lands on the target σ′n. The reference scan missed it because the root lies between its last inadmissible and first admissible grid points.

Metamorphic checks

  • Scaling the slope, sphere and σci together leaves FoS unchanged to 1.1 × 10−15 (EJC and GHB, dry and wet, 3D and 2D).
  • With φ = 0, pore pressure has no effect: dry, water-table and ru runs give identical FoS.
  • With φ = 0, FoS is proportional to c: doubling and quadrupling c give ratios of 2.000000000000000 and 4.000000000000000.
